Concall Summary - Feb 2026

Deepak Nitrite (Q3 & 9M FY26) Concall Summary — Key Operational + Strategic Takeaways
1) Operating backdrop: pricing pressure, China-led oversupply, volatile trade flows
Management framed the environment as “persistent pricing pressures, heightened competitive intensity and an uneven demand pattern”, driven by “protectionism and aggressive pricing by Chinese producers”.
Despite this, the group’s near-term posture is cautious but improving, with management expecting Q4 FY26 to be better than Q3 FY26, and explicitly adding “Q1 FY27 will be better still.”
2) Consolidated performance: volumes up, margins transitioning; cash discipline highlighted
Q3 FY26 consolidated revenue: INR 1,983 crore (+3% YoY / +3% QoQ)
Q3 EBITDA: INR 219 crore (+16% YoY); EBITDA margin 11% vs 10% last year
Q3 PBT: INR 151 crore (+12% YoY) includes one-time exceptional provision INR 12.84 crore (new labour codes)
9M FY26: revenue INR 5,820 crore, EBITDA INR 658 crore, PAT INR 331 crore
Mix: Domestic/Export 83:17 in Q3 (domestic revenue INR 1,647 crore, exports INR 328 crore).
Capital efficiency: ROCE 15% (consolidated).
Strong emphasis on working capital: “tighter inventory planning, supply chain optimization and improved receivables discipline” to enhance cash flows and balance sheet quality.
3) Segmental: Phenolics stable/strong; Advanced Intermediates (AI) growth but profits squeezed
Phenolics

Q3 revenue: INR 1,334 crore
Q3 EBIT: INR 145 crore (+20% YoY)
Drivers: higher phenol/acetone volumes (utilization + process optimization), and management cited “better net raw material realization, finished goods margin” plus efficiency gains.
They reached a milestone: “Today, we manufactured our 2 millionth tonne of phenol.”
On pricing/spreads: management cautioned Q4 volatility (China New Year + India year-end), saying any view may be “outdated within the next week.” However, they indicated India-specific procurement dynamics helped margins versus global indices.
Advanced Intermediates (AI)

Q3 revenue: INR 652 crore (+18% YoY / +11% QoQ) on volume and penetration.
Q3 EBIT: INR 15 crore (severely impacted), attributed to “continued pricing pressure arising from global oversupply… aggressive import competition”.
A crucial nuance: management admitted part of the margin pressure was tactical/self-inflicted—they prioritized market presence and utilization even at weaker contribution:
They were “aggressive in the supply… into the market” and in some products may have “softened it a little bit more” (example: para-nitroaniline).
AI margin recovery thesis is tied to newly commissioned upstream integration (see next section).
4) Integration milestone: nitric acid + nitration + hydrogenation chain; Q3 hit due to commissioning mismatch
A major operational development was completion of vertical integration across ammonia → nitration → amines:

Commissioned: WNA & CNA nitric acid plants at Nandesari, plus nitration and expanded hydrogenation at Dahej.
Management positioned this as structural: “completes our vertical integration across the ammonia-nitration-to-amines value chain… enhances raw material security and structurally strengthens our margins through reduced supplier dependency.”
They described themselves as “probably the only completely integrated aromatic-to-amine player.”
Why Q3 margins were weak (key disclosure):

There was a timing mismatch: nitration/ETP commissioned earlier, but nitric acid commissioning slipped to mid-Dec’25 due to “technical challenges in the last mile.”
Strategic choice: continue high operating rates and buy nitric acid at spot rates, hurting contribution:
“maximize our market presence even if it comes at the expense of having to procure raw materials like nitric acid at spot rates… Q3 looked much weaker.”
Benefit timing: nitric acid started mid-Dec’25; management expects this quarter and next quarter to be first periods with “pretty much 100% consumption of nitric acid” (i.e., internal).
5) Sodium nitrite—US anti-dumping removal (company-specific) and volume implications
Newsworthy: US removal of ADD is “specific for Deepak Nitrite… not… for Indian manufacturers.”
Historical exports to US: ~5,000 tonnes/year for the last few years.
Prior tariff burden: combined ADD + reciprocal duty created “in excess of about 105%” overhang between ex-factory India and US landed.
Now: ADD reduced to “essentially zero”; reciprocal duty down to 18% (from 50%); CVD stays (~3–5% range mentioned).
Commercial implication: they are re-engaging customers to regain/expand share, with focus on value discipline (not just price-led).
6) New products pipeline: 15 products; application focus; staggered commercialization
Management disclosed a pipeline of ~15 products across stages (R&D, pilot, customer sampling/feedback).
Target applications: mining chemicals, flame retardants, personal care, flavors & fragrances, polymer applications.
Commercialization: most (except polymer application requiring “medium-sized CAPEX”) are at pilot scale with plant mapping completed; awaiting validation cycles (“purity profiling and stability studies”) and customer approvals.
Timing: “next several months in a staggered manner” (implied FY26 end into early FY27), depending on approval stages.
7) Project execution / ramp-up timelines: what reaches steady state when
Management gave relatively granular ramp guidance by asset:

Nitric acid + nitration + hydrogenation: expected ~95–105% utilization from this quarter onwards.
Photochemical fluorination / chlorination / fluorination: utilization pickup expected in Q1 FY27; photochemical fluorine starts towards end of this quarter.
Fluorination (commissioned May): gradual ramp aligned to customer requirements; earlier issues cited around BTC quality, expected to resolve; BTC→BTF and similar chemistries expected from Q1 FY27.
MIBK/MIBC: commissioning targeted “within this quarter”; ramp in Q1 FY27 (phased). They emphasized process uniqueness and downstream optionality.
High-pressure alkylation asset: expected around June–July 2026, ramp 2–3 months.
Management clarified some capex is upstream integration (no direct revenue)—notably nitric acid—intended to lift AI EBITDA structurally.
MIBK/MIBC delay explanation (important process insight):

They denied a “two-year delay” but admitted delay due to adopting a non-standard, high-efficiency design:
“we… would be the only… company… [to use] a divided wall column… there were a lot of hiccups… took longer.”
They used the time to optimize catalyst and recyclability, and began seed marketing of downstreams in advance.
8) Polycarbonate (PC) mega-project: timeline, execution status, and competitive framing
PC project positioned as transformational: India’s first integrated propylene → polycarbonate chain.
Status: Stade, Germany dismantling underway; engineering/technology/site construction progressing.
Timeline reaffirmed: commissioning targeted December 2027 (+/- a couple months).
Funding: “financial arrangements and funding tie-up are at the final stage.”
Competitive stance vs European incumbents (incl. EU-India trade deal concerns): management does not see FTAs eroding the integrated India advantage; they emphasized India demand growth (imports rising materially vs their original estimates) and their integrated chain (propylene/phenol/BPA/PC).
9) Demand signals and where management sees “green shoots” (vs China-exposed categories)
They explicitly said 60–70% of products are not as directly affected by Chinese oversupply, though customer economics can be impacted indirectly.
Areas cited for potential uplift (linked to trade shifts + underlying demand): dyes & pigments, mining chemicals, explosives, polymer applications, food applications, pharmaceuticals, personal care.
Agrochemicals: “marginal improvement… not consistent… still… rocky”, dependent on product, geography, and weather; impact for intermediates tends to lag 1–2 quarters.
Explosives/defence linkage: highlighted strong collaboration with customers to “meet and exceed military grades”; expects outcomes in 3–6 months as customers invest.
10) Capex and financial structure: near-term spend and balance sheet
FY26 remaining capex: ~INR 100 crore, taking FY26 total to ~INR 1,200–1,300 crore.
FY27 capex: ~INR 2,500 crore.
Net worth disclosed: INR 5,651 crore (consolidated).
Cost lines: Q3 finance cost INR 11 crore; D&A INR 58 crore (post capitalization of new assets like nitration/hydrogenation).
11) Management’s stated operating doctrine and near-term playbook
“Three core mantras”: “world’s best quality… world’s best capacity utilization… complete integration across the value chain.”
Near-term operating strategy is explicitly utilization- and integration-led:
They chose to protect/expand market presence during commissioning even at margin cost, but now aim to restore pricing discipline: “now that we have our upstream integration… improve the prices where it doesn’t look like… we are in such a hurry to push.”
Outlook framing: optimistic on integration benefits and improved trade stance (US sodium nitrite ADD removal; potential US/India/EU tailwinds), but still recognizes persistent China oversupply as a structural headwind for some lines.
