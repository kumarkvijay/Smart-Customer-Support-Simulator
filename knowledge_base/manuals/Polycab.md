Concall Summary - Jan 2026

Polycab India Ltd — Q3 FY26 Concall Transcript Summary (Jan 16, 2026)
1) News / Governance update (material)
Board approved redesignation of Bharat Jaisinghani and Nikhil Jaisinghani from Executive Directors to Joint Managing Directors, effective immediately, subject to shareholder approval. Management positioned this as continuity and reinforcement of leadership: “deep understanding of the business, strategic clarity, and hands-on leadership… key contributors to the Company's success.”
2) Consolidated financial performance — Q3 FY26 (operationally relevant drivers)
Revenue: +46% YoY (led by Wires & Cables; FMEG also healthy).
EBITDA: +34% YoY; EBITDA margin 12.7%.
One-off: ~₹219m gratuity provisioning due to new labour code; excluding this, margin would be ~13%.
PAT: ₹6.3bn (highest ever Q3), +36% YoY; PAT margin 8.3%.
Balance sheet: net cash ₹30.3bn.
Working capital: 27 days at Q3 end. Inventory higher due to deliberate build for Q4; payable days also higher (use of LCs). Management expects normalization to “50 to 55 days” steady-state “in the coming quarters.”
Capex: ₹3.4bn in Q3; ₹10.9bn in 9M FY26, aligned with Project Spring capex intent (₹12–16bn annually through FY30).
9M FY26 (record performance):

Revenue +30% YoY, crossed ₹200bn.
EBITDA +47% YoY, margin 14.2%.
PAT +47% YoY, margin 9.6%.
3) Wires & Cables (W&C): growth quality, mix, market-positioning, and margin mechanics
Topline and volumes

Segment revenue +53% YoY in Q3.
Domestic W&C: +59% YoY revenue; ~40% volume growth (management: “volume growth is about 40%”).
Growth composition/mix:
Wires outperformed cables in revenue terms, attributed to channel pre-stocking amid elevated copper.
Within cables, institutional sales outpaced channel sales, indicating project-led demand traction.
Market share / competitive stance

Management repeatedly signaled market share gains, but avoided quantification since peers hadn’t reported yet. Still, they framed the quarter as “further strengthening of our market position” with “continued market share gains,” enabled by Project Spring execution (channel relationships, market execution).
Demand drivers highlighted

Broad-based: government + private capex and real estate.
Government capex: cited ~28.2% YoY surge in first 8 months to ₹6.6tn; also mentioned ₹1.25tn loan to states for capex.
Real estate: launches/sales in top cities near decade highs; affordable housing recovery noted.
Channel dynamics: wires saw inventory build at distributors; channel inventory moved from ~30 days typical to ~40–45 days (i.e., +10–15 days incremental), but management stressed strong secondary/tertiary offtake and expects Q4 absorption.
Profitability pressure: explicit root causes

Key issue: sharp commodity inflation + FX, with a deliberate pass-through strategy:
Sep–Dec 2025 (INR terms): copper +~21% QoQ, aluminium +~11% QoQ.
Longer window cited: Jan’25–Jan’26 copper ~+50%, aluminium ~+25%; Apr–Dec copper +35%, aluminium +27% (including INR depreciation).
Strategy: staggered pass-through to avoid demand disruption and protect volumes/relationships:
Management: “took a strategic decision to pass on… in a staggered manner,” accepting “near-term margin pressure” to “protect volumes, gain market share and further strengthen relationships with our channel partners.”
Pass-through progress: ~75–80% of the commodity inflation already taken within/around the quarter; remainder to be passed “during this quarter” (Q4), with some hikes already implemented partially in January.
Mix headwinds also mattered:
Institutional mix up (typically ~90:10 distribution:institutional; institutional share improved “by about a couple of hundred basis points”).
Exports contribution down (management cited 8.3% of topline last year Q3 vs ~6% this Q3), which hurt margin mix.
Margin framework and expectations

Company reiterated W&C profitability is managed as percentage margin, not “rupees per ton.”
Long-term W&C guidance referenced under Project Spring: “11% to 13%” (near term historically ~12–14% referenced by analyst; management acknowledged near-term range discussions but reiterated long-term 11–13%).
Q4 sequential: management expects margins to improve vs Q3 (“should definitely get better”), conditional on passing through remaining commodity inflation; they did not guide commodity direction.
Capacity / supply

W&C capacity utilization: “early 80% range” in Q3.
4) International business (exports): steady but US tariff headwind
International revenue +5% YoY, contributes ~6% of consolidated revenue.
Geography: growth driven by non-US markets (“Middle East, Latin America” cited).
US softness explicitly attributed to tariffs: “Yes, it is tariff related… awaiting a final resolution.”
Management said order book is healthy and expects continuation of growth trajectory in coming quarters.
5) FMEG: growth cadence, profitability inflection, category drivers, and A&P posture
Performance

FMEG revenue +17% YoY in Q3; management emphasized consistent industry outperformance aligned with Project Spring aspiration (1.5–2.0x industry growth).
Profitable for the 4th consecutive quarter, despite higher A&P; management expects profitability expansion with scale and reiterated FY30 target 8–10% EBITDA margin for FMEG.
Solar: standout driver

Solar grew “more than 2x” YoY in Q3, driven by central/state rooftop incentive scheme uptake.
Outlook: continued strength expected; solar described as “already the largest contributor… in the FMEG segment”.
Margin: solar at “high single digit” margin (reaffirmed).
Product/portfolio: referenced launch of “350-kilowatt solar inverters” in the prior quarter and said it “did very well.”
Fans: regulatory transition + demand sensitivity

Fans performance in Q3: industry “largely flattish or a small degrowth”; Polycab in line.
Drivers: weaker summer and channel inventory liquidation in Oct–Nov; some Dec pickup due to BEE transition effective Jan 1.
Forward view: pricing expected to rise due to new BEE norms, with price hikes “2% to 4%” in first 1–1.5 months of CY26; demand heavily dependent on next summer season.
A&P (Advertising & Promotion): stepped-up but still below medium-term intent

Q3 A&P higher (investor noted ~3x quarterly run-rate). Management framed seasonality (festive + celebrity associations).
Importantly, despite the spike, management said A&P is ~1.5% of B2C topline currently vs their stated ambition to move to “3% to 5% of B2C top-line every year” over time; second half tends to be heavier (Q4 also elevated due to pre-summer).
6) EPC: BharatNet execution begins; margin profile reiterated
EPC revenue ₹4,069m, +4% YoY.
Began execution of existing BharatNet orders:
₹4.5bn over next 3 years (project execution)
plus ₹3.5bn over 10 years (O&M)
Segment profitability ₹272m, margin 6.7%.
Sustainable EPC operating margin expected to remain “high single digits” mid-to-long term.
7) Management’s near-term posture: what changed vs “normal” and why it matters
Deliberate margin trade-off for share/volume is the key operational stance this quarter. Management repeatedly characterized staggered pass-through as strategic and relationship-focused, not execution slippage:
“such high 35% passing… can’t be done every month… hence… staggered manner”
Aim: avoid demand disruption, sustain momentum, and reinforce channel loyalty.
Channel pre-stocking is acknowledged (primarily wires), but management’s base case is continued absorption due to strong underlying end demand and seasonally strong Q4 execution.
Export mix headwind (lower export contribution and US tariff disruption) is a meaningful swing factor to monitor, though management cited healthy order book and diversified growth.
8) Useful disclosed operational metrics / mix points (investor reference)
Domestic W&C volume growth ~40% YoY (both wires and cables similar in volume growth).
Revenue growth split (domestic): wires ~70%, cables ~50% (inflation effect).
Mix: typical cables:wires = 70:30, but wires share increased “a few percentage points” in Q3.
Sales mix: typically distribution:institutional ~90:10, institutional up by ~200 bps this quarter.
Export share: ~6% this quarter vs ~8.3% in Q3 FY25.
Cable demand end-use: Power T&D indicated as ~30% for Polycab (for cable demand contribution, as asked).
