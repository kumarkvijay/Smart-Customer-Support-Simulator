Concall Summary - Feb 2026

Context and purpose of the call
Call was convened to clarify financial and operational implications of a disclosed fraud after investors indicated the exchange filing was “quite detailed, but still the impact… is not well understood,” particularly whether the amount will “be hit to the P&L” and the role of recoveries.
Incident summary: what happened, where, and with whom
Location / scope: Management repeatedly emphasized this is “confined to a particular branch in Chandigarh” and limited to “a limited set of Haryana government linked accounts”; “one branch, one client group.”
Nature of fraud: Alleged employee collusion with external parties, involving non-digital, physical instruments:
“debit instructions have come supposedly from the client” and were processed fraudulently.
Use of “cheques, when… looks forged, but someone has cleared it.”
CEO characterized it as “the oldest kind of fraud probably known to banking.”
Control override via collusion: Despite maker-checker-authorizer, “obviously… some bunch of people have come together to make it happen.”
Quantum, exposure, and potential P&L impact (as currently understood)
Discrepancy identified:
INR 490 crores: from reconciliation when Haryana-linked entities sought to transfer/confirm balances; mismatch between what departments believed vs bank account balances.
Additional INR 100 crores: bank’s self-identified estimate from scanning other related accounts; described as “a bit of our own estimate… but we think we’re broadly right.”
Total disclosed potential impact: INR 590 crores, communicated as a best-faith “outer limit” to avoid drip disclosure: “we really don’t want to postpone and leak the market with more and more news.”
Insurance mitigant: Bank has employee dishonesty insurance of ~INR 35 crores.
Recoveries: Management is tracing funds “layer by level one, level two, level three,” and will “block the money and try to recover” where still within the system; recovery actions and lien marking initiated.
Provisioning stance: CEO: “We have not yet decided what we will provide or not provide… but… please assume that we will take some provisions for this.”
Liability allocation not finalized: Management repeatedly avoided definitive answers on who ultimately bears losses, noting it may become legal and will depend on forensic outcomes and responsibilities across parties (bank, officials, beneficiaries, receiving banks).
Detection trigger, timing, and why it surfaced now
Trigger event: The matter “significant[ly]” surfaced when Haryana government departments requested fund transfers / balance confirmations, exposing reconciliation gaps.
Timing clarity provided (limited):
CEO suggested the mismatch became visible “probably… a month ago” but actions “accelerated during the last three days from February 18th onwards.”
Management declined to detail full duration given legal sensitivity.
Actions taken: governance, investigation, law enforcement, and recovery
Immediate personnel action: “All employees whom we currently suspect have been suspended.”
Independent forensic audit: KPMG appointed; scope being finalized; expected duration ~4–5 weeks (“typically… could take about four to five weeks”).
Governance escalation: Fraud management committee, Board, and Audit Committee discussions held; regulators and auditors informed.
Legal / enforcement: Police complaints filed; engagement with law enforcement agencies ongoing.
System-wide recovery actions: “initiated recovery and lien marking actions across the banking system.”
Bank-to-bank coordination: Funds went to “many other banks”; those banks are “cooperating.”
Controls and process commentary: what existed, what failed, what changes are planned
Controls asserted to already exist
Maker-checker-authorizer framework, SOPs, authorization framework.
High-value confirmation calls: For “transaction value above INR5 lakhs, the branch staff calls the customer to confirm.”
“Positive Pay” option: Customer can pre-confirm cheque details in app (opt-in; not mandatory).
Additional verification above INR10 lakh: “additional verifier verifies” correctness.
Customer communication controls: System-generated “SMS alerts… monthly bank statements… monthly balance confirmation” were sent to registered IDs.
Why controls did not prevent this (management view)
Not a system glitch: “There is absolutely no system error”; statements reflected true ledger balances after the fraudulent debits.
Core failure mode is collusion: “many of these people connived in making it happen.”
Incremental controls planned (explicitly described)
Mandatory digital customer confirmation for high-value branch transactions above a threshold:
“make it mandatory” and captured “through a verified digital channel with a stipulated window” (app-based authorization rather than phone call reliance).
AI-assisted cheque scrutiny: AI performs initial signature/cheque checks, then “double confirmed by the human,” aiming improved exception handling.
Broader introspection on collusion risk controls, including:
“staff transfer policy”
“transaction pattern… employees’ accounts and conduct”
Deeper methods “to prevent collusion”
Deposit and franchise implications (government + overall)
Haryana government deposits: ~0.5% of total deposits; outflow since notification ~INR 200 crores; management views this as non-material vs total deposits of ~INR 2.8 lakh crores.
Total government/PSU deposits: “about 8% to 10%” of deposits (central + state + PSU).
Relationship depth as mitigation: Bank stressed government business is not passive deposits but “deep solutions” and “host-to-host integrations,” including tax collections (GST/CBDT/state taxes), implying stickiness.
De-empanelment risk: Haryana’s reaction seen as “natural”; bank intends to “win back the confidence.” Management did not report adverse signals from other government entities: “Nothing… apart from what has come from this… department, nothing else.”
Broader deposit franchise confidence: CEO argued deposits are supported by a “full stack” (product, tech, service, culture), and one-branch incident should be looked through.
Profitability, capital/liquidity posture, and near-term outlook (management framing)
Ability to absorb shock: CEO emphasized the bank is “fundamentally in a strong position” and can “subsume” the outcome.
Profit trajectory assertions (pre-incident baseline):
“operating profit… has now crossed 2%” vs “earlier 0.5%”
Expectation of NIM improvement: last quarter “5.7%,” “this quarter we expect… 5.8%”
Expectation that credit cost will keep coming down
CEO: absent this issue, Q4 expected to be a “very solid quarter”; profit had increased from ~INR 350 crores to ~INR 500 crores over last two quarters, and they expected a higher number in Q4.
Profitability despite provisions: “despite this hit… we still expect to be profitable” (as of call-date estimate).
Conservatism on liquidity/funding: Bank claims “super, super conservative” posture:
Incremental CD ratio ~75% (7-year average)
Very low reliance on certificates of deposit
Will monitor liquidity; no explicit indication of materially raising buffers beyond current practice.
Newsworthy takeaways / actionable investor lenses
Magnitude and accounting: Management publicly frames INR 590 crores as a prudent “one-shot” estimate; final P&L impact depends on recoveries + insurance + assignment of liability.
Forensic timeline: A tangible near-term catalyst is KPMG’s 4–5 week forensic process, after which clearer responsibility/loss allocation may emerge.
Control changes are concrete: Planned shift from phone-based confirmation to mandatory app-based digital authorization for high-value branch transactions; plus AI + human dual validation for physical cheque scrutiny.
Key risk reframed: Management repeatedly positions this as collusion risk, not system weakness—important for assessing whether investors should extrapolate to broader operational fragility.
Franchise confidence vs headline risk: Bank acknowledges reputational disruption (“deeply disturbed us”) but argues underlying earnings momentum (NIM up, credit costs down) and capitalization allow the issue to be “looked through” in ~one quarter, while it internally will not be forgotten (“this incident will not pass through our memory”).
